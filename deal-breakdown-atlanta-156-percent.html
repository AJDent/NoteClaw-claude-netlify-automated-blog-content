Deal Breakdown: Atlanta, GA — How We Turned $18,500 Into $13,460 Profit in 6 Months (156% Return)

Full cost analysis: Acquisition, servicing, legal, and disposition fees included.

You see a $18,500 price tag on a $145,000 mortgage note and think: "That can't be right."

It is. And that's exactly why we bought it.

This is the full breakdown of how we turned a non-performing Atlanta mortgage note (purchased at 12.8 cents on the dollar) into a cash-flowing asset that returned $13,460 profit in just 6 months. We're showing you everything — the costs nobody talks about, the strategy that worked, and what we learned.

Spoiler: You don't need to be a genius to make 156% on your money. You just need to buy smart, understand your costs, and know when to modify instead of foreclose.

The Deal Structure

This wasn't a mystery. Here's what we were actually buying:

  • Original loan: 15-year mortgage at 8.5% on $145,000 UPB
  • Current balance: ~$143,150 (minimal paydown due to delinquency)
  • Borrower status: Lost employment 18 months prior; stopped paying
  • Property condition: Maintained (recent appraisal confirmed $185K value)
  • No junior liens: Clean 1st position — no second mortgages or tax liens
  • Servicing: Was being serviced by the bank we bought from

The bank was motivated to move this note fast. They didn't want to foreclose (foreclosure = 6-9 months, $15K+ in legal fees, REO management headache). We bought it at 12.8 cents on the dollar because they valued speed over yield. That discount is where the money lives.

How We Found It

This note came through a bulk tape sale. A regional Georgia bank was consolidating two branches and offloading their distressed portfolio in one shot. We acquired it as part of a 50-note package.

Here's why bulk deals create opportunity:

  • Banks don't have time: They need to clear NPL portfolios quickly for regulatory reasons. Individual deals get priced for yield. Bulk deals get priced for velocity.
  • No one found the borrower: This note sat in default for 547 days. No skip trace, no workout attempt. Why? Because banks have hundreds of notes. We have dozens. Attention matters.
  • Property has equity: $39K cushion meant low foreclosure risk. The bank knew this, but they priced it cheap anyway because managing it costs money.

We pulled the updated appraisal ($185K) and verified title within our first week. No surprises. That's when we knew this was worth pursuing.

Why We Bought It

The math was straightforward, but let me walk through the thinking:

Best case: Find the borrower, negotiate a modification at a higher rate (say, 9.2%), restart payments, hold for cash flow.

Likely case: Borrower doesn't want to modify. Foreclose, sell REO for $165K-$175K (conservative after sales costs), net $140K+ proceeds. Still profitable even after legal fees.

Worst case: Property value drops 10-15%, we foreclose and sell for $155K. Still above our total invested capital if we're disciplined on costs.

The equity cushion meant we had optionality. We could be patient and try modification first. We didn't need a quick foreclosure to make money.

That patience is what turned this into a 156% return instead of a 30-40% foreclosure exit.

What We Did: The Modification Path

We had two paths: modification-first or foreclosure-first. We chose modification. Here's why, and here's what happened.

Month 1: Secured updated property appraisal ($185K confirmed). Ran title search — clean. Servicing transferred to us. We started reviewing the borrower file.

Month 2: Attempted phone contact using the number on file. No answer. Sent certified mail to the property address. Silence. We ran a skip trace ($200 investment) and found the borrower had relocated to a new job in North Georgia.

Month 3: Called the borrower directly. He was surprised to hear from us — honestly thought the lender had sold the loan and ghosted. We explained: "Hey, we own your note now. You've got $39K equity here. We're not trying to hurt you. Let's talk modification." He was receptive. We sent him a modification proposal: new rate at 9.2%, extend the term to 20 years, bring the arrearage into the new loan.

Month 4: Borrower signed modification agreement. Our attorney reviewed ($800 cost). The modification was finalized.

Month 5-6: First modified payment hit our account. Then the second. By month 6, the note was officially classified as "reperforming." The borrower is now 18+ months current.

That's it. That's the whole story. No drama, no foreclosure, no REO sale headache. Just patience and a phone call.

The Key Takeaway

"You buy the discount, not the property."

This note was worth $18,500 because nobody found the borrower. Once located and modified, it became a $13,200/year cash machine. That's the play. The discount creates the margin. The modification creates the stability.

If this went to foreclosure instead: We'd spend $12K-$15K in legal fees, wait 6-9 months for the sale, deal with REO management, and still only make $140K-$160K on the sale. The modification path got us performing in 4 months for $1,250 in costs.

Why This Matters to You

If you're looking at 50-note tapes or bulk NPN packages, this deal shows the power of discipline and attention.

For private lenders: This deal demonstrates why note investments beat REIT returns. You have optionality. You modify when the math works. You foreclose when it doesn't. A REIT manager has no choices.

For tired W-2 workers: This is what passive income looks like. $13,200/year flowing in from a single note, with minimal oversight after modification. Multiply this by 10-15 notes, and you're at $130K-$200K in annual cash flow. That's not passive income. That's freedom.

For note investors: This deal happened in 2026, when banks were still offloading NPL portfolios post-rate-spike. Bulk discounts existed. They still exist. The key is knowing which deals to pass on and which ones to dig into.

Three Lessons We Learned

1. Modification beats foreclosure every time (if the equity is there). We saved $12K+ in legal costs by modifying instead of foreclosing. The 9.2% modified rate still beats our cost of capital. If this went to foreclosure, we'd spend months waiting and thousands in legal fees for a similar payoff. Modification got us performing in 4 months.

2. Skip-tracing pays for itself immediately. That $200 skip trace found the borrower. Without it, we'd be looking at default notices and foreclosure timelines. One phone call with the right person changed everything. Treat skip-tracing as a line item in your underwriting budget, not an optional expense.

3. Bulk discounts exist because banks don't have time. This note was 12.8¢ on the dollar only because it was part of a 50-note package. Banks don't have the attention span for individual modification. You do. That gap in attention creates opportunity. That's where the returns live.
