03 · You fund the position · YOU

Your capital funds a specific note against a specific property. You see the collateral, the lien position, the equity cushion, and the same file we used to price it. Retirement accounts fund through your custodian.

04 · We buy at a discount · TNC

We buy below what is owed, and below what the house could sell for. That gap is what protects your money, and it is the reason we can be patient with a borrower instead of rushing the process.

08 · What else are people asking?

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is a mortgage note, and what do I actually own?

A mortgage note is the legal document a borrower signs for a home loan: the amount, the rate, the schedule, and what happens if they stop paying. Invest in it and you step into the lender's seat. You become the bank. You do not own the house, you own the debt secured by it, with a legal claim to the property if the borrower stops paying. That is what makes it cleaner than being a landlord.

Can I invest using my IRA or 401(k)?

Yes, and it is one of the main reasons investors use notes. A Self-Directed IRA or Solo 401(k) can hold mortgage notes, with the interest flowing back tax-deferred or tax-free. Your account becomes the lender, so the note is titled to your custodian. You will need a custodian that allows alternative assets. One IRS rule: your account cannot lend to you or close family. We walk through all of it on the call.

How is my investment protected if something goes wrong?

Every note is secured by a physical property, and we buy at a strict discount, typically 30 to 60 cents on the dollar at 70 percent loan-to-value or less. That equity buffer is your first layer of protection. If a borrower defaults, you have recourse: modification, deed-in-lieu, or foreclosure. We also check for tax liens first, since they outrank the mortgage. Skipping that is a common rookie mistake. We do not.

What happens if a borrower stops paying?

We do not panic, we have a playbook. First we work with the servicer to reach the borrower. Often a loan modification, a lower rate or a longer term, gets them paying again and turns the note back to performing. If that fails, we pursue deed-in-lieu or foreclosure through our attorneys. Timelines vary by state, from months to a couple of years. The real estate behind the note stands behind your position the whole way.
