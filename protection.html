How your money is protected

The price we pay is what protects you.

No forms here, nothing to sign. Just how we decide what to buy, the cushion behind every note, and what happens when a borrower stops paying.

What we check before we buy anything.

Most of what we look at, we pass on. This is the list that decides. If a note fails the first group, nothing else matters and we stop reading.

Group 1

Instant stops
  • Is the lien secured, and in what position
  • Has the statute of limitations run in that state
  • Is the property underwater with no path to equity
  • For a second lien: is the senior lien already in foreclosure
  • Will the seller provide the basic data at all

Group 2

The numbers
  • Loan to value against a value we trust, not the seller's
  • What we would pay as a share of what is owed
  • How many months since the borrower last paid
  • A property value we can stand behind, refreshed if stale

Group 3

The file
  • Original note and mortgage, recorded assignments, clean chain
  • Title search for liens, judgments and transfers we were not told about
  • Property taxes current, or how far behind
  • Bankruptcy, active loss mitigation, or servicing notes on file

Group 4

Can it resolve
  • Occupancy status and whether the borrower has ever engaged
  • Foreclosure timeline and cost in that specific state
  • At least two exits that realistically work
  • What the recovery looks like after every cost comes off
A note has to have at least two workable exits before we bid. One exit is a gamble. Two is a strategy plan. Three is a game plan.

We describe the checks here, not the thresholds. The exact bands and state tables are in the 60-Second Tape Filter. This page proves there is a process; the guide gives you the process.

What happens when a borrower stops paying.

A note is not a single bet on one outcome. There is a ladder of ways this can end, and we work them in order.

1

Reinstatement

The borrower brings the loan current, paying the missed payments plus fees, and the loan continues exactly as written. The cleanest outcome, and more common than people expect.

2

Repayment plan

The borrower cannot clear the arrears all at once, so the past-due amount is spread across future payments on top of the regular payment until they are caught up.

3

Loan modification

We change the rate, the term, or both to a payment the borrower can actually make. The most common workout, and the one that turns a non-performing note back into a performing one.

4

Discounted payoff

The borrower or a family member settles the loan for a lump sum that is less than the full balance. We take the cash and release the lien.

5

Short sale

With our approval, the borrower sells the property for less than what is owed. We recover from the sale proceeds and the borrower avoids a foreclosure on their record.

6

Deed in lieu

The borrower voluntarily signs the property over to us instead of fighting a foreclosure. Faster and cheaper for everyone than a contested case.

7

Foreclosure

The floor, not the plan. If nothing else works, we recover through the collateral itself, the property that secured the loan from day one. Timelines and costs vary by state.

Multiple exit paths. Three outcomes. Zero dead ends.

Roughly half of our notes re-perform, about a quarter exit voluntarily, and about a quarter reach foreclosure. Foreclosure at about one in four is not rare. It is planned for, priced in, and secured by the property the whole way.
