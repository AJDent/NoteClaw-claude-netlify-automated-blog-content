The Problem

Your 401(k) is probably underworking.

Most 401(k) plans give you a menu of a dozen mutual funds and a "target date" fund that assumes you want average returns. That's fine for someone who's never heard of alternative assets. It's not fine for you.

🎯

Cookie-Cutter Menus

Your plan offers 10-15 funds. None of them buy real estate. None of them lend on property. All of them charge fees regardless of performance.

📊

Market-Correlated

When the stock market drops 30%, so does your retirement. Notes are backed by physical property. A very different risk profile.

🔒

No Real Control

You picked "Growth Fund C" once in 2014 and haven't looked since. A Solo 401(k) puts you in the driver's seat with checkbook control.

Solo 401(k) vs. SDIRA

Which structure fits you better?

Both let you invest in notes. Each has advantages. Here's the plain-English breakdown.

Solo 401(k)

Best for self-employed

  • ✓Contribute up to $69,000/yr (2026)
  • ✓Checkbook control. No custodian delays
  • ✓Loan up to $50,000 to yourself
  • ✓UBIT exemption on debt-financed real estate
  • ✓Roth option available
  • ✗Requires self-employment income
Self-Directed IRA

Best for W-2 investors

  • ✓Available to anyone with earned income
  • ✓Roll over old 401(k)s and IRAs easily
  • ✓Roth conversion strategies available
  • ✓Simpler admin than Solo 401(k)
  • ✗Contribution limits capped at $7,000-$8,000
  • ✗Custodian sign-off required per deal

Not sure which fits you? That's covered in the guide, and we can figure it out on a 15-minute call.
